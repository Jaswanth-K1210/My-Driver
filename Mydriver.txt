2. The Problem We Are Actually Solving
The driver-on-demand market in India has already solved supply. DriveU has 100,000+ verified drivers
and did ₹111.86 Cr gross platform revenue in FY25, profitably.

What nobody has solved is visibility after the car leaves.
The three failure moments

Moment 1 — 11:40 PM, Jubilee Hills.
A woman has had two drinks. She books a driver. He is a stranger, it is her car, and she is alone in the
back seat. Her only safety tool is an SOS button she has to be conscious and calm enough to press.

Moment 2 — 6:10 AM, RGIA Departures.
₹300–500 a day for airport parking. She sends the car home with a driver instead — and then spends the
entire flight with no idea where her car actually is, how it is being driven, or what condition it will be in.

Moment 3 — 4:30 PM, school / hospital run.
A driver takes her parents across the city. She gets a text on arrival. Everything between pickup and
drop is a black box.


The gap in one line

   Existing platforms give you a moving dot on a map.
   A dot cannot tell you whether anything is wrong.

Our product is not "a driver app with tracking." It is an accountability system that happens to include a
driver.

4. Core USP — Phase 1 vs Phase 2

4.1 The strategic decision
The original plan led with the dashcam. That was the right instinct and the wrong sequence. Three
reasons to move the camera to Phase 2:

   1. The camera is buyable. KENT CamEye already sells live in-cabin video and GPS to any consumer
     off the shelf. Uber has already rolled out in-cabin recording in Hyderabad. Leading with the
     camera means leading with a commodity.
   2. The camera is the expensive, slow, legally hardest part. Hardware CAPEX, 4G SIMs, streaming
     cost, DPDP consent architecture, driver resistance — all of it front-loaded before we know
     whether anyone will pay.
   3. The camera is not the moat anyway. The moat is the verification and escalation system around it —
     and that system works perfectly well in Phase 1 with nothing but two smartphones.

  Strategy: ship the trust layer first, add the lens second.
  Phase 1 proves people pay for accountability. Phase 2 makes accountability visual.

5. Phase 1: The Digital Guardian Stack
  This is the answer to "what replaces the dashcam?"
  Ten software-and-operations features. Zero hardware. All ten are things DriveU does not do
  today.




5.1 Dual-GPS Trip Integrity Engine ★ core moat
What it does: Continuously reconciles three signals — the driver's phone GPS, the customer's phone
GPS (when riding), and the expected route baseline from the map engine.

It watches for:

     Route deviation beyond a tolerance corridor
     Unscheduled stops longer than 3 minutes
     Driver app force-closed, location permission revoked, phone powered off
     Speed sustained above the customer-set ceiling
     Trip start/end location mismatch against the booking

Escalation: in-app warning → SMS + IVR → Safety Desk human → structured escalation.
Why it's the moat: A safety feature the driver can silently switch off is theatre. A system where
switching it off is itself the alarm is a safety product. This logic carries forward unchanged into Phase 2
— the camera simply becomes a fourth signal.




5.2 Guardian Link
What it does: At trip start, a live tracking link auto-goes to up to 3 pre-set contacts.

What makes it different from Uber's share-trip:
Uber gives your contact a link they have to choose to look at. Guardian Link pushes alerts to the
guardian:

      "Trip started. Priya is in the car."
      "⚠️ Route deviation detected — 1.4 km off expected path."
      "⚠️ Car stationary for 6 minutes at an unscheduled location."
      "✅ Arrived. Priya confirmed safe."

Insight: the person most motivated to act is often not the passenger. It is the person waiting at home.
Nobody is building for them.




5.3 Live Driver Behaviour Score
What it does: Uses the driver's phone accelerometer, gyroscope and GPS to score every trip on:

                     Signal                                            Detected via

 Harsh braking / acceleration                   Accelerometer delta

 Aggressive cornering                           Gyroscope

 Overspeeding vs road limit                     GPS + map speed-limit data

 Phone handling while moving                    Screen-on + motion correlation

 Night fatigue pattern                          Hours driven + micro-swerve detection

Rolls into a 0–100 MyDriver Score, visible to the customer before they accept a driver, and to us for
driver ranking and payout tiering.

Why it's different: DriveU has star ratings. Star ratings measure politeness. This measures driving.




5.4 Customer-Set Speed Ceiling
The customer sets a maximum speed at booking (e.g. 60 km/h in city, 90 on highway). Breach triggers:

   1. Audible alert in the driver app immediately
   2. Push notification to the customer
   3. Logged permanently to the Trip Vault
   4. Three breaches in a trip → auto-flag to Safety Desk

Works entirely off the driver's phone GPS. Zero hardware. This is the single highest-value feature for the
airport and family use cases and it costs us nothing.




5.5 Pickup Handshake (Identity Lock)
The problem nobody talks about: a verified driver's account being used by an unverified person — a
friend, a brother, a substitute. This is a real and documented failure in Indian driver platforms.

Our fix, at every single pickup:

   1. Driver takes a live selfie in-app — liveness check, not an upload
   2. Face-matched against the onboarding photo
   3. Customer confirms a 4-digit OTP with the driver
   4. Trip cannot start until both pass

Result: we can honestly say "the person driving your car is the person we verified." DriveU cannot say that
today.




5.6 Car Condition Capture
Before the trip starts and after it ends, the driver app forces an 8-point photo capture — timestamped,
geotagged, non-uploadable-from-gallery:

Front · Rear · Left · Right · Dashboard (odometer + fuel) · Front seats ·
Rear seats · Boot

Protects the customer: proof of pre-existing vs new damage.
Protects the driver: proof he did not cause it.
Protects us: kills the single most common dispute in this business.

This also becomes the Phase 2 bridge — the same capture flow later runs on the VisionCam.




5.7 Night Shield Protocol (10 PM – 5 AM)
A hard operating protocol, not a feature toggle:

      Only Night Shield certified drivers are allocated (6+ months tenure, MyDriver Score ≥ 85, zero
      open complaints, re-verified every 90 days)
      Fitness check-in at shift start — liveness selfie + a 10-second reaction test
      Every active night trip appears on the Safety Desk live board, watched by a human
      Guardian Link is mandatory, not optional
      Silent SOS via triple volume-button press
      Safe-Arrival check-in: if no confirmation within 10 minutes of drop, the Desk calls

This is the product for Test Case 01, and it needs no camera at all.




5.8 Trip Vault
Every trip generates an immutable, tamper-evident record:

Route polyline · Speed profile · All stops with duration
All alerts fired · Driver identity + selfie hash
Condition photos (before/after) · Odometer + fuel deltas
Payment record · Escalations, if any

Exportable as a signed PDF. Retained 90 days by default, 24 months for corporate accounts.

Why it matters: this is what makes us sellable to corporates, usable for insurance claims, and credible if
a matter ever reaches the police. It is also, quietly, a defensible data asset.

6. Phase 2: MyDriver VisionCam

6.1 The adaptive-orientation model
The original always-on inward camera is removed. Instead, a single motorised/repositionable dual-lens
unit that the customer configures per trip:


Mode R — Road Only (default)

Camera faces forward. Cabin is not captured at all.
Use when: the owner is in the car and wants zero cabin recording.


Mode D — Driver Focus

Frame is tightened to the driver's seat only. Rear seats and passengers are outside the frame.
Use when: the owner or family are riding and want the driver accountable without being filmed
themselves.

   This is the direct answer to "if the owner doesn't want himself in the cam."


Mode F — Full Cabin

Wide interior framing showing the whole cabin.
Use when: the car is travelling empty — airport drop, service run, car delivery. No passenger to record,
so full visibility of the car's interior and contents.


Mode-switch logic

Booking type = EMPTY CAR                         → default Mode F
Booking type = OWNER RIDING                      → default Mode D
Owner privacy toggle ON                          → force Mode R
Owner can change mode mid-trip, from the app, any time.
Every mode change is logged to the Trip Vault.
Critical design rule: the customer can reduce capture at any time. The driver can never change the
mode. Only the Safety Desk can widen it, and only on a verified L4/L5 escalation.


6.2 What VisionCam does NOT do
❌ No two-way voice. Removed.
❌ No cabin audio recording of any kind.
❌ No 24×7 recording — streaming is on-demand only, when the customer opens the app.
❌ No footage retention beyond 14 days unless the trip is flagged.
❌ No mode the driver controls.
6.3 The device constraint that drives everything
Our driver drives a different customer's car on every single trip. An installed dashcam is impossible.
The unit must therefore be:

     Portable — carried by the driver, car to car
     Self-powered — internal battery, 12V as backup
     Self-connected — its own 4G M2M SIM, independent of the customer's car
     Fast-mounting — suction/magnetic, under 90 seconds
     Cheap enough to lose — 10–15% annual loss/breakage must be survivable

Indicative unit cost ₹8,000–10,000 all-in (Qubo 4G Live class), plus ₹300–600/month per device for SIM,
streaming and cloud.



6.4 VisionCam + the Integrity Engine
In Phase 2 the camera becomes a fourth GPS signal. The Integrity Engine now also detects: device
powered off, SIM removed, lens obstructed, camera GPS diverging from phone GPS. Same escalation
ladder, more signal. The Phase 1 architecture does not get rebuilt — it gets extended.

12.4    ⚠️ Correcting the original projection
The original document stated: 50 drivers × 10 trips/day × ₹80 = ₹40,000/day, then concluded monthly
revenue "can cross ₹1 lakh."

Two errors:

   1. ₹40,000/day is ₹12 lakh/month, not ₹1 lakh — a 12× arithmetic gap.
   2. 10 trips per driver per day is not achievable. A hired-driver trip averages 3 hours. Ten of them is
      30 hours in a day. Realistic month-1 utilisation is 1–1.5 trips per driver per day.

Honest Month 1: 50 drivers × 1.2 trips = 60 trips/day × ₹126 contribution ≈ ₹7,560/day ≈ ₹2.3 lakh/month.
Plan on that. Building a budget on the fantasy number is how startups run out of money in month 7.
